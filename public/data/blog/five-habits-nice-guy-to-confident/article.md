## Overview

In this short video, filmed from the driver's seat of his car, BasedMike lists five habits he says took him from a "nice guy" in his early 20s to a confident, masculine man in his 30s and beyond. He says up front that the list is in no particular order. He isn't saving the best for last, and he treats all five as equally important.

One idea runs through all five habits. **Real confidence comes from doing uncomfortable things repeatedly and finding out you survive them.** No object, substance or shortcut gives you that. Each habit is a different way of collecting that proof.

## Habit 1: Be Assertive

Don't be afraid to **project your personality into the world**: your opinions, your viewpoint, your likes and dislikes. Do it in ordinary conversations and in conversations with women.

His reasoning:

- **It makes you stand out.** He argues that most people never say what they really think, or have no real opinion at all, so they have nothing to say that sets them apart.
- **The feared consequences rarely show up.** Make a habit of saying what you think, even when it's a bit unconventional or slightly taboo, and you'll find there's usually no real fallout.
- **Proof builds confidence.** Once you've seen that "everything's fine" after putting yourself on the line, your confidence grows.

He adds a common-sense caveat. Being assertive doesn't mean walking up to a stranger in a nightclub and insulting his hair or face. "I assume you've got the intelligence to differentiate."

> Put your opinions out there and let the chips fall where they may, and you will see that everything's fine.

## Habit 2: Learn to Enjoy Discomfort

More precisely, learn to enjoy **the discomfort that comes with progress**: stepping outside your comfort zone and learning new things.

Anything you've never done before will feel uncomfortable, even painful, because you're bad at it and it feels like resistance. His advice is to **keep doing it badly**. Everyone is bad at a new thing the first time, and repetition makes you better.

He compares it to training hard in the gym. A hard set hurts, and at first you link that pain with nothing good, so you ask, "Why am I doing this to myself?" The benefit only arrives over time. Once you see progress, you start **associating the discomfort with the progress it brings**.

He gives two reasons this works:

- "Practice makes perfect." Practice may not make you perfect, he says, but it makes you "a hell of a lot better" than when you started.
- Humans **adapt and overcome**. He credits that ability with our survival and with making us the planet's apex predator.

## Habit 3: Lift

Go to the gym, set a training routine and **stick to it**.

- **Beginners need structure.** Show up at least **three days a week**. Even three half-hour sessions are "plenty" if you push hard on every exercise. That's enough to build an impressive physique, "if it's in your genetics."
- **Advanced lifters can loosen up.** Once you know the basics and how to train, you can drop the strict routine.

He lists the benefits:

- **Physical and mental.** Lifting improves both.
- **With women.** He argues a fit body shows you care about yourself and have dedication, and that women find this attractive.
- **Confidence.** It "bleeds through into nearly every other area of your life."
- **Protection.** Strength means you can defend yourself and the people you love.

He expects some pushback: "We don't live in caveman times, nobody needs to be strong." His answer is that "shit happens," people get assaulted, and you can't know what situations life will bring. You want to be prepared for anything.

## Habit 4: No Crutches

**Build your confidence on your own**, without leaning on anything external.

His main example is alcohol. Plenty of men drink on a night out to find the nerve to talk to women and act how they really want to. It might work in the moment. The problem comes when you're with a woman somewhere alcohol isn't available. Do you carry a flask and sip it on the sly to stay buzzed? "That's going to lead to really bad things."

Alcohol isn't the only crutch. He also names:

- A really nice car
- Designer or expensive clothes
- An expensive watch you show off when you go out

Anything that gives you **situational, borrowed confidence** is "a fool's game," because you won't always have it with you. What you want is **core confidence** that doesn't depend on a situation, an object or a chemical, and is "instilled within you."

He also asks whether you really want your confidence, and so your happiness, to depend on something outside you. He never used crutches, because he thought the fastest route to confidence was doing it alone:

> Just throw yourself into the flames and let it burn away all the shit that you don't want to be there.

He ties this back to the gym. **The more discomfort you accept at the start, the faster you progress.** Push through the hardest part of the set and eventually you reach where you wanted to be and stay there. "It'll just be who you are. You won't need anything."

## Habit 5: Drastically Limit Your Porn Intake

He is clear about what he's *not* saying:

- **He rejects NoFap.** He calls total abstinence "way overkill." He thinks resisting completely makes it into "some huge thing that it doesn't need to be" and causes problems you don't need.
- **He thinks regular release is healthy.** He says men need release regularly, weekly or every two weeks but not every day, "for the health of your body and for your prostate."
- **Imagination over porn.** If you can manage with your imagination alone, he thinks that's much better than watching porn.

He gives three reasons porn hurts confidence:

1. **It affects your brain.** He says it delivers a "massive flood of dopamine" and does strange things to your brain that won't help you build confidence.
2. **It sets unrealistic expectations.** "Porn is a fantasy world." Watching "perfect people" sets false standards.
3. **It removes your motivation.** If you're constantly rewarding yourself on your phone or computer, you have no reason to go out, build confidence and meet a real woman.

**His rule:** wait **at least a week** between sessions, or better, two weeks. He thinks this will boost your motivation to meet real women and make you feel better about yourself. Just knowing you have the willpower to wait that long "is going to help your confidence right from the start."

## Key Takeaways

- **Say what you think.** Share your opinions and likes openly. The fallout you fear rarely comes, and seeing that builds confidence.
- **Make friends with discomfort.** Keep doing new things badly until you're good at them. Learn to see the discomfort as a sign of progress.
- **Lift at least three times a week.** Even 30-minute sessions work if you train hard. The confidence carries over to the rest of your life, and strength helps if you ever have to defend yourself.
- **Drop the crutches.** Alcohol, cars, designer clothes and watches give you borrowed confidence that disappears in situations without them. Build confidence that goes with you everywhere.
- **Limit porn, but don't swing to NoFap.** Wait at least a week, ideally two, between sessions. Imagination beats porn.
- **It's all one mechanism.** Every habit is a way of building confidence through repeated discomfort.
