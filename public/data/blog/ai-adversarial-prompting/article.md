A man died of a heart attack in June. During his four-hour emergency room visit, he racked up a $195,000 medical bill. His brother-in-law took that bill to Claude AI and had a conversation about which billing codes were legitimate.

The result? The AI identified $162,000 in Medicare billing violations that the hospital couldn't defend. The bill dropped from nearly $200,000 to just over $30,000.

This isn't just a story about one family getting relief from an outrageous medical bill. It's a story about how AI is fundamentally changing the power dynamic between individuals and institutions that have long relied on information asymmetry to extract money from people who don't understand complex systems.

## The Hidden Weapon: Institutional Information Asymmetry

The hospital was counting on the widow and her family not knowing the billing codes. They were counting on them not understanding Medicare bundling rules. They were counting on them not having $3,000 to hire a medical billing advocate, so they would just pay or go bankrupt trying.

That's less true now with AI.

This pattern extends far beyond medical billing:

- **Debt collectors** count on you not knowing statutes of limitations
- **Funeral homes** count on you not knowing FTC regulations when you're grieving
- **Insurance companies** count on you not understanding policy language when they deny your claim
- **School districts** count on you not knowing procedural timelines when your kid needs services

![Information Asymmetry](/blog/ai-adversarial-prompting/screenshot-1.jpg)

Institutions don't accidentally make things confusing. They construct information asymmetry on purpose because complexity is how they charge differential prices to different people based on your ability to navigate the system.

They're intentionally creating mazes that ring bells for money. That has worked for a long time because there has been no map to the maze.

AI changes that.

## Why Investigation Used to Cost Thousands

We all have to face the school system, the medical system, the legal system at some point. Investigation used to cost thousands of dollars in these situations. Medical billing advocates charge $3,000 or more. Lawyers charge hundreds of dollars an hour just to understand your case.

Those costs make fighting back prohibitively expensive for most disputes, and institutions count on it.

AI collapses that cost from thousands of dollars to about three hours of your time. But only if you understand the right approach.

## The Critical Reframe: Investigation, Not Advice

This is where the model makers are actually right when they emphasize: do not go to AI for advice.

But here's the reframe no one talks about: You're not using AI to get advice. You're using AI to help you conduct an institutional-grade investigation.

There's a methodology to how that works. And if you're in a situation where you're facing one of these institutions and they're betting on you to be confused, angry, grieving, and unfocused, AI can help.

## The 8 Principles of Adversarial Prompting

There are eight specific capabilities that make LLMs uniquely powerful in adversarial contexts. An adversarial context is where the institution is out to bill you or take advantage of you in some way.

AI becomes a weapon that helps you level the playing field and even out the informational asymmetry these institutions depend on.

### 1. Parse Technical Frameworks Built to Intimidate

Have the LLM read technical documents designed to be unreadable by humans:

- Medicare billing rules
- FDCPA statutes
- IEP regulations
- Insurance policy appendices

This matters because institutions are betting you won't do this. But AI is not intimidated by jargon. It decodes these documents quickly because it's trained on them.

You can audit these institutions' compliance with their own rules without subject matter expertise.

### 2. Cross-Reference Multiple Authority Sources

Check if CPT codes were billed correctly against:
- CMS bundling rules
- Medicare fee schedules
- Setting requirements

![Cross-Reference Documents](/blog/ai-adversarial-prompting/screenshot-3.jpg)

Violations hide in the gaps between documents. A hospital can bill procedure X in setting Y differently than in setting Z. You have to check bundling rules, fee schedules, and more.

This kind of multi-document pattern recognition is extremely hard for humans to hold in their heads. AI is exceptionally good at it.

### 3. Match Institutional Registers

Register refers to the formal way of speaking that matters for navigating systems. If you can speak in a formal register of English, you're more likely to get what you want.

The convenient thing about AI: You don't have to speak that formal register yourself.

LLMs can draft correspondence that reads like it came from someone who does this professionally:
- Cite regulatory citations accurately
- Measure escalation threats appropriately
- Signal sophistication in approach

Institutions triage disputes by sophistication. An angry consumer letter gets ignored safely. A documented violation with professional cadence is a very different thing.

### 4. Identify the Applicable Rulebook

Use LLMs to figure out what technical framework governs your domain:

- Hospital billing → Medicare rules
- Debt collection → FDCPA and state statutes of limitations
- Special ed services → IDEA and case law

![Rulebooks](/blog/ai-adversarial-prompting/screenshot-4.jpg)

You cannot audit compliance without knowing which rulebook applies. Most people don't know that every domain has documented standards or where to find them.

The unlock: Have the AI hunt up the rulebook, tell you what it is, find the current copy, and compare it to your situation.

### 5. Find True Categorical Violations

Identify clean, clear binary violations of the rule. Either they did X or they didn't. Avoid subjective disputes like "it seems expensive."

This matters because "your bill is too high" is an opinion they can safely ignore. But "you billed bundling codes separately, violating CMS regulation X" is a categorical violation they can't defend.

Examples:
- Instead of "the casket price seems really high" → "FTC funeral rule 453.3 prohibits requiring purchase from a particular provider"
- Instead of "my kid needs more support" → "Evaluation shows standard scores of X, comparable students receive Y services, proposal fails FAPE standard under IDEA"

LLMs help you find where your particular situation breaks a category that exists in the rulebook.

### 6. Calculate Objective Anchors from Authoritative Standards

Establish a defensible position based on published benchmarks:
- Medicare reimbursement rates
- Comparable property sales
- Required clinical guidelines

![Benchmarks](/blog/ai-adversarial-prompting/screenshot-5.jpg)

Your position should not be "I can't afford this" or "this doesn't seem fair." It needs to be what the standards establish.

Example: "Medicare would reimburse $30,000 for this procedure. That's our offer."

The hospital can't argue Medicare rates are unreasonable without admitting Medicare loses them money.

### 7. Collapse Investigation Costs While Maintaining Verification Control

AI can identify potential violations quickly, but you must verify findings that carry risk.

When model makers say "AI doesn't give medical or legal advice," they mean the AI can't take legal liability for verifying findings. You are responsible for verification.

That's actually fine, because AI can do everything up to that step:
- Identify potential violations
- Explain why they're violations
- Explain how to respond
- Draft proposal letters

Then you assess: Is this actually correct?

It's very easy to take Regulation X, type it into Google, and check that you're citing something real. That takes two seconds instead of paying hundreds or thousands to a specialist.

In adversarial contexts, the stakes are higher. Wrong citations signal you don't know what you're talking about.

### 8. Draft Verification Prompts to Catch AI's Own Mistakes

You can actually use AI to check its own work:
- Check dispute letters
- Flag citation errors
- Check incorrect code interpretations

![Verification](/blog/ai-adversarial-prompting/screenshot-6.jpg)

Most people complaining about AI hallucinations have never written a verification prompt. If you're using AI to do institutional-grade investigation, use AI to install safeguards.

This doesn't absolve you of verification responsibility, but it helps you go faster.

## The Underlying Principles That Make This Work

Beyond the eight tactical principles, there are deeper strategic understandings at play:

### Investigation Must Precede Negotiation

Your instinct when you get an unfair bill is to start negotiating. But AI can help you reframe your emotions and get into investigation mode first. That's critical to actually winning.

### Control the Frame of the Conversation

The hospital might say "we offer charity assistance to people who can't afford their billing." But that frames the pricing as legitimate.

Your reframe: "We don't seek charity. We are negotiating based on documented billing violations."

Now the hospital is on weaker ground. They have to defend why they broke the rules.

AI can help you recognize framing attempts and draft responses that refuse the frame.

### Responses Are Diagnostic

People think they send a letter and either the institution settles or they don't. But what actually happens tells you about the strength of your position:

- **They fold immediately** → They can't win. Take the win.
- **They ignore you** → They're either bluffing or your position is weaker than you thought. Evaluate honestly.
- **They counter reasonably** → You're in negotiation territory. Decide if the gap is worth fighting for.

You can use AI strategically to understand the intelligence coming from the other party and adjust your position.

## The Bigger Picture: A Revolution in Power Dynamics

We live in a system where institutions have historically had an exclusive monopoly on complex information.

They don't anymore.

AI levels the playing field on information complexity. But most people don't know how to use AI to actually level that field because, like everything else in AI, it's how you use it that matters.

If you just say "hey Claude, give me advice on this bill," you'll get much less value than if you methodically follow this step-by-step process.

The way we use AI shapes our ability to access the expertise inside the model. The model has this expertise. It has tools to get current documents. But if you don't know how to ask for it, you're stuck with a very generic "can you help?"

![Power Shift](/blog/ai-adversarial-prompting/screenshot-7.jpg)

As far back as we can look in history, institutions have had more power partly because they manage complex information. We are at a point where individuals can level that playing field.

This is much bigger than a story about someone being charged a ridiculous amount for an ER visit. This is about institutions everywhere trying desperately to hold on to a monopoly on information that AI is eating away.

Let's make sure that what we are charged, that what we are offered, reflects the fair standards that are written down. AI helps us get there. AI helps us get to a more just world.
