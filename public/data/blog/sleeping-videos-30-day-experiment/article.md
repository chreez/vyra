## Overview

Sleep videos are a deceptively powerful YouTube niche. Because sleeping viewers never pause, skip, or close the tab, watch time — YouTube's most important ranking metric — accumulates at an extraordinary rate. One video in the niche has racked up 500 million views. Assuming a modest $1.50 RPM with a single pre-roll ad, that single video earned roughly $784,000 over 10 years. It's still pulling around 5,000 views per hour — or about $750 an hour.

Inspired by those numbers, creator Nion Anarchy spent 30 days building a sleep video channel from zero — renaming a dormant BeamNG Shorts channel to "Santai Deep Sleep Sounds" — and documenting every step: keyword research, production, thumbnail testing, failed hacks, and one viral joke video that outperformed everything else. The results were modest but revealing, and the experiment exposes exactly what it takes to crack this niche.

## Keyword Research: The Difference Between Visible and Invisible

The first rule of sleep videos is to never compete in oversaturated terms. "Rain sounds" has enormous search volume, but so does its competition — your video disappears instantly.

Using vidIQ's keyword tool (which shows monthly search volume and a competition score from 0–100), the goal is to find the sweet spot: high volume, low competition.

"Rain cabin" is a textbook example. Over 200,000 monthly searches, competition score of 16.2 — nearly empty. Compare that to "thundersleep": fewer than 5,000 monthly searches and a competition score of 38.1. The math is obvious.

![Keyword research with vidIQ](/blog/sleeping-videos-30-day-experiment/keyword-research-tool.jpg)

The posting plan built from this research covered four themed weeks: rain sounds, natural landscapes, object sounds, and — intentionally — a "shit-posting" week of absurd content to test whether novelty could beat quality.

## Production: 10-Hour Videos Are Non-Negotiable

YouTube's search autocomplete almost always suggests "10 hours" for soundscape searches. Viewers want full-night coverage. Anything shorter gets passed over.

This creates a production bottleneck: 10-hour videos are massive files, often 50GB+. When your monthly internet plan caps at 300GB, uploading six videos eats your entire quota. The solution: find a nearby shopping center with free Wi-Fi and camp there.

For visuals, free stock footage from Pexels and Pixabay works — but requires careful looping to prevent jarring 30-second stutters. For the first video (rain cabin), no free footage met the bar, so a custom Blender 3D forest scene was rendered instead.

Thumbnails matter more than you'd expect for audio-only content. YouTube lets you A/B test three thumbnail variants simultaneously, then reports which drives the highest click-through rate. Even among "rain cabin" videos, view counts correlate directly with thumbnail quality. The product being sold is an emotion — it should look like a fairy tale.

![A/B thumbnail testing results](/blog/sleeping-videos-30-day-experiment/thumbnail-ab-testing.jpg)

## Week 1: Rain Videos Underperform

Day 1: 24 views, $0.05.

Over the full first week — rain cabin, rain on tin roof, rain on car roof, rain on tent — the channel accumulated 152 views, 3 subscribers, and $0.14. At that rate, annual revenue would be roughly $7.28.

One tactic attempted during this week: adding multilingual title and description translations (via Google Translate) to target high-RPM regions. YouTube lets you add alternate-language versions of your title and description without subtitles — a Polish viewer would see the Polish title. The targets were Western Europe, North America, Australia, and New Zealand. (New Zealand gets excluded from so many ad campaigns there's an entire subreddit about it.)

![The Reddit maps-without-New Zealand reference](/blog/sleeping-videos-30-day-experiment/reddit-maps-localization.jpg)

The localization strategy showed no meaningful impact. The rain on car roof video — the one that received the most translation work — ended up being the worst-performing video on the channel.

## Week 2: Forest Content Changes Everything

Week 2 shifted to natural landscapes. The forest ambience video was an immediate outlier: unlike rain videos that died after two days, it kept climbing steadily. Notably, the creator was so confident in one particular thumbnail image that he skipped A/B testing entirely and just ran it — the one exception to the usual three-variant test.

![Day 10 forest waterfall — first video to cross 100 views](/blog/sleeping-videos-30-day-experiment/day-10-breakout-video.jpg)

Forest waterfall on day 10 became the first video to cross 100 views and earn $0.16. By the end of week 2, cumulative revenue crossed $1 — the clearest signal yet that forest outperformed rain.

A side experiment: the rain on tin roof video had unexpectedly high watch time (43 minutes average vs. 2–5 minutes for other videos). The plan was to cut it from 10 hours down to 3 hours in YouTube's internal editor, artificially inflating the watch time percentage from ~7% to ~30%. The technique came from The Spiffing Brit.

![19% watch time display](/blog/sleeping-videos-30-day-experiment/watch-time-metric.jpg)

It failed completely. The video got 2 views after the edit, then flatlined. YouTube appears to have patched this exploit since The Spiffing Brit's video was published.

Week 2 final stats: 785 views, 14 subscribers, $1.05.

![Week 2 summary card](/blog/sleeping-videos-30-day-experiment/week-2-stats.jpg)

## Week 3: Object Sounds — And a Delayed Explosion

Week 3 was pre-scheduled and uploaded in bulk before leaving for a festival in Germany. The planned content: clock ticking, cat purring, fireplace crackling, and similar household sounds.

None of them broke out. Clock ticking led the week with 46 views. Cat purring underperformed expectations.

But while away, the forest ambience video from week 2 quietly went viral in relative terms. It crossed 1,110 views and earned $1.47 — the channel's first 1,000-view video, arriving three weeks after upload. Sleep video growth is non-linear; older videos can spike long after posting.

## Week 4: The Viral Joke Video

The final week was built around deliberate absurdity. The hypothesis: plenty of stupid YouTube videos pull millions of views, so why not test it?

The centerpiece: a 10-hour video of a stock image of a 2003 Toyota Corolla, with failed startup sounds looping for the entire duration — and one successful startup sound hidden at the very end. The title included "(it starts at the end)" with a timestamp.

Within one day: 4,000+ views. Nearly $6 earned.

The joke content massively outperformed every serious video on the channel. Construction noises and wall drilling each got decent views. Chainsaw noises — despite the comedic premise — failed: too abrasive to tune out, it landed in the channel's bottom three overall.

![Day 25 channel analytics](/blog/sleeping-videos-30-day-experiment/day-25-channel-analytics.jpg)

The watch time manipulation hack was confirmed dead this week too. After cutting the rain on tin roof video, it flatlined immediately.

## Final Results and Long-Term Outlook

After 30 days and 20 videos:

- **~7,500 total views**
- **~$10 earned**
- **Projected annual revenue: ~$113**

Top 3 videos: Toyota Corolla startup joke, forest ambience, washing machine at 1200 RPM.

Bottom 3: chainsaw noises, TV static, rain on car roof.

The channel reached neither 500 subscribers nor 3,000 watch hours — the thresholds for YouTube Partner Program monetization on a new channel. The monetization was possible only because the channel had been previously monetized as a BeamNG Shorts channel.

The real potential of the niche lies in long-term compounding. The forest ambience video, three weeks old, is still climbing. Sleep video channels typically see older videos spike unpredictably, accumulating views for years. The $784,000 example took 10 years to get there — and it's still earning.

## Key Takeaways

- **Pick obscure keywords** — Use vidIQ or similar to find high-volume, low-competition terms. "Rain sounds" is a graveyard. "Rain cabin" is an opportunity.
- **Make it 10 hours** — YouTube autocompletes "10 hours" for soundscape searches. Anything shorter loses discoverability.
- **Forest beats rain** — In this experiment, forest ambiance consistently outperformed rain sounds in sustained view growth.
- **Thumbnails drive clicks even for audio content** — A/B test three options and let the data decide.
- **Watch time manipulation is patched** — Trimming video duration to inflate watch time percentage no longer works.
- **Joke content can outperform serious content overnight** — The Corolla video outperformed 19 serious videos combined in a single day.
- **Localization showed minimal ROI** — Multilingual titles and descriptions didn't move the needle in 30 days.
- **Patience is the actual strategy** — Sleep channels compound over years, not weeks. Month 1 results are not predictive of year 1 results.
